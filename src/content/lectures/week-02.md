A generated clip is a five-second unit of work, not a scene: most models
lose coherence past that length, so the discipline this week is treating
one shot as the entire assignment. The craft question is how to make that
one shot say everything it needs to, and let the model's control points do
the work rather than the prompt alone.

## Keyframe-first control

Text-to-video generates from words alone, giving weak control over
composition and none over what a character or set looks like from shot to
shot. Image-to-video reverses the order: generate or select a still frame
first to lock composition, framing, and art direction, then write a short
motion description whose only job is to steer what happens next. Locking
the frame before writing the motion is this week's single biggest
improvement in control, and why every exercise from here starts with a
still.

## Four things your prompt should say

A shot's grammar collapses into four decisions, and each earns roughly one
sentence in the prompt: who or what the subject is and what it's doing, how
close the shot is — the shot size, from wide to close-up — what the camera
itself is doing while the subject acts (static, pan, push, handheld), and
what the light is doing (source, direction, hardness). Write those four
sentences in order and you have a usable prompt before you've thought about
style at all.

## Reading a bad take

When a generated shot fails, it tends to fail in the same three places
first: hands, physics, and any text in frame. Extra or fused fingers,
objects that pass through each other, and garbled signage or captions are
the tells that a take isn't usable, and they're worth checking before
anything else — a shot can have perfect framing and light and still be
unusable because of a hand in the background.

## Before class

Have one still frame ready — generated or otherwise — that you're willing
to build a shot around.

## This week's exercise

Turn that still into one five-second shot using an image-to-video workflow,
with a prompt written as the four sentences above. Produce three takes,
reject at least one of them in writing using the hands/physics/text
checklist, and bring the still, all three takes, and your rejection note to
Wednesday's Dailies.
