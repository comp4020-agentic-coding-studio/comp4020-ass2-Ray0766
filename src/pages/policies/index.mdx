## Extensions and late submission

Ask before the deadline. A short extension for a specific,
stated reason is usually granted; the same request made once the deadline
has passed is an explanation, and gets assessed as one.

Work submitted late without an approved extension loses 10 percentage
points of the marks available for that piece for every day it is late.
Days are whole calendar days counted from the deadline, weekends and
public holidays included, and any part of a day counts as a day. Ten days
of that takes the whole piece, so work more than ten days late is recorded
as zero rather than marked.

The arithmetic once, so nobody has to guess at it: a 3% exercise that
would have scored 2.4 and arrives two days late loses 0.6 of the 3 marks
available, and is recorded as 1.8. The deduction comes off the mark the
work earned, not off the mark it might have earned on time.

## Academic integrity, for a course built on generated footage

Every frame produced for this course is assumed to be AI-generated by
default. That is the premise the course starts from. What
counts as misconduct here is what counts as misconduct anywhere: presenting
someone else's work as your own, or fabricating the evidence meant to prove
what you did. On this rig, that means a production log describing a workflow
you did not run, or a seed and parameter set that does not reproduce the shot
it's attached to, is the offense — not the fact that a model made the
picture.

## Likeness and portrait rights

Do not use an identifiable, recognizable face of a real living person —
yourself or anyone else — without that person's informed consent, on this
course or on any platform you might publish to afterward. This follows
ordinary personality-rights and right-of-publicity law, and the
synthetic-media policies most publishing platforms already enforce:
generating a face does not exempt it from the rules that already apply to
using one. Reference material you don't hold clear rights to does not become
usable because a generative model touched it.

## Dailies etiquette

Watch a take all the way through before saying anything about it. Half a
screening produces a note about the half you watched. Feedback addresses
the shot, the log, and the reference; the person who made it is out of
scope. "This drifted from the reference" is a note. A remark about whoever
ran the generation has no place in the room.

## Equipment and compute

Hardware in this course is whatever you bring; the school does not guarantee
any student a specific card or a specific amount of VRAM. Where the school
provides a shared render window on its own machines, bookings are
first-come, and a booking you don't show up for is released to the next
person waiting, without appeal.
