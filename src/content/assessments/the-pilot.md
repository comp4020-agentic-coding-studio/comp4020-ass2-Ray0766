> One episode, hooked in three seconds and held together for the rest of
> it.

## What to do

Produce one finished pilot episode, sixty to ninety seconds, framed for
a vertical screen: a beat sheet written in states, a storyboard with a
sized shot list, every shot generated against a locked model sheet and
scene master plate, run through the quality gate, then assembled with
loudness matched to the published standard and captions present and in
sync. The opening beat is the episode's three-second hook, and the rest
of the beat sheet has to earn what that hook promised.

## What to submit

The assembled pilot, delivered to the platform's spec with captions
burned in; the beat sheet and storyboard it was shot from; the model
sheet and scene master plate used to hold continuity; and a production
log covering every gated shot, in the order they appear.

## How it's assessed

Hook and pacing checks whether the opening three seconds earns the rest
of the episode, and whether beat density holds across the full run
rather than flattening out partway through. Continuity checks that
every shot matches the model sheet and master plate it was audited
against. Delivery compliance checks loudness against the published
standard and captions for presence and sync, on a phone screen. Craft
covers execution quality more generally. Production log is checked the
way every log this semester has been: legible enough to rerun a shot
without asking.
