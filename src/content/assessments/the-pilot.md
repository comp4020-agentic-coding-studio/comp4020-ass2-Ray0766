> One episode, hooked in three seconds and held together for the rest of
> it.

## What to do

Produce one finished pilot episode, sixty to ninety seconds, framed for
a vertical screen: a beat sheet written in states, a storyboard with a
sized shot list, every shot generated against a locked model sheet and
scene master plate, run through the quality gate, then assembled with
loudness matched to the published standard and captions present and in
sync. The opening beat is the episode's three-second hook, and the rest
of the beat sheet has to earn what that hook promised.

## What to submit

The assembled pilot, delivered to the platform's spec with captions
burned in; the beat sheet and storyboard it was shot from; the model
sheet and scene master plate used to hold continuity; and a production
log covering every gated shot, in the order they appear.

## How it's assessed

Hook and pacing checks whether the opening three seconds earns the rest
of the episode, and whether beat density holds across the full run
rather than flattening out partway through. Continuity checks that
every shot matches the model sheet and master plate it was audited
against. Delivery compliance checks loudness against the published
standard and captions for presence and sync, on a phone screen.
Production log is checked the way every log this semester has been:
legible enough to rerun a shot without asking.

### Craft — 20% of this assessment

Craft assesses the finish of the selected shots and their assembly.
Use the [shot checklist](/lectures/week-02/#the-ladder), the
[patch-or-reshoot decision](/lectures/week-10/#patch-or-reshoot), and
[sound through the edit](/lectures/week-11/#sound-and-captions-carried-through)
to review the submitted cut:

- **Framing and action:** the intended subject and action remain readable
  on a phone. Cropping does not accidentally hide a gesture or the object
  the shot needs the viewer to see.
- **Take selection and repair:** distracting generation faults, such as
  a hand changing shape during an action or unintended text in the image,
  have been rejected or repaired. A patch does not leave a visible edge
  or flicker that creates another distraction.
- **Assembly finish:** no stray frames or accidental audio clicks remain
  at edits; dialogue stays intelligible when music and effects are added.
  Review the transitions in the assembled cut, not only the source clips.

A strong submission controls these details throughout; any remaining
imperfection is minor and does not obscure the action. A satisfactory
submission is readable but has isolated distracting faults or rough
joins. A weak submission repeatedly leaves those faults in the finished
cut, making the action or dialogue difficult to follow. An intentional
stylised effect is judged by whether it serves the shot, not by how
closely it resembles a photograph. More renders or a more expensive
model do not earn credit by themselves.

Keep the criteria separate: whether the opening earns attention and the
sequence holds it belongs to Hook & pacing; matching a character or
location across shots belongs to Continuity; measured loudness, caption
presence and sync, and export requirements belong to Delivery compliance.
A fault is not deducted again under Craft simply because it was found
under one of those criteria. Craft uses the pilot already submitted;
there is no additional reel or written defence to produce.
