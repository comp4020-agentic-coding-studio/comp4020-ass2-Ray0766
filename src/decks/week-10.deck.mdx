## This week adds

The production line: a shot queue, roles split across it, and a gate every
shot clears before it leaves.

```notes
Everything before this week produced one shot, one scene, or one reference set
at a time. An episode is dozens of shots on a deadline, and that difference is
not a matter of degree — it needs a queue, split roles, and a gate every shot
has to clear, none of which a single person working alone actually needs.
```

---

## The queue and the roles

One role generates, one audits continuity, one handles edit and sound. Shots
move through in order, rather than each person running their own process
end to end.

```notes
A production line works because no one person carries a shot from prompt to
finished frame alone: one role generates from the model sheet and master
plate, one role runs the continuity audit against them, one role handles the
edit and sound pass, and shots move through that queue in order rather than
each person running their own end-to-end process. Splitting the work this way
is what makes an episode's worth of shots possible on a deadline instead of
one shot at a time, indefinitely.
```

---

## Patch or reshoot

Patch when the log says the setup is reproducible and the flaw is local.
Reshoot when the log itself is what's broken.

```notes
At episode scale, a flawed shot is a cost decision as much as a craft one.
Patching — fixing one wrong hand, one drifted prop — is cheap but only fixes
what's local to that shot; a reshoot is a full new render at a full render's
cost. The decision comes down to the log: patch when the log says the setup is
reproducible and the flaw is small, reshoot when the log itself is what's
broken and nothing local will fix it.
```

## The form you are shooting into

<p class="deck-figure">664 million<span class="deck-figure__unit">micro-drama viewers, 59.0% of all netizens</span></p>

This is not a niche the course invented. CNNIC's December 2025 count puts the
micro-drama audience at 664 million people, against 1.125 billion netizens
overall.

<p class="deck-source">Source: CNNIC, 57th Statistical Report on China's Internet Development (Feb 2026), 核心数据, p.9 — https://www.cnnic.net.cn/NMediaFile/2026/0304/MAIN1772588317069TUXN3827X8.pdf</p>

```notes
The citation used to point at the report's release page, which announces the
report and does not carry this number. The sentence is on page 9 of the PDF,
under 核心数据: "网络视频用户规模达 10.93 亿人……其中，短视频用户规模达 10.74 亿人，
占网民整体的 95.4%；微短剧用户规模达 6.64 亿人，占网民整体的 59.0%。" The 11.25
billion netizens it is measured against is the first bullet on the same page.
Cite the page that holds the number, not the page that holds the link to it.

The direction matters as much as the number. CNNIC's June 2025 report had the
same audience at 626 million and 55.8% of netizens; December 2024 had it at
662 million and 59.7%. So the form is enormous and no longer growing quickly,
which is a different production problem from a form still finding its
audience.

For scale beside a familiar number: the industry white paper reports a 2024
micro-drama market of about 50.44 billion yuan, and the film regulator reports
2024 box office at 42.502 billion yuan. Those are two separate primary
sources, so "micro-drama passed the box office" is arithmetic done here rather
than a claim either source makes.
```

## The gate is not a course invention

Since 1 June 2024 a micro-drama that has not been filed and reviewed may not
be distributed online, and budget is the main line between the tiers.

| Tier                       | Reviewed by                            |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Key, at or above ¥1,000,000 | regulator: filing and finished programme |
| Ordinary, ¥300,000-1,000,000 | regulator: filing and finished programme |
| Other, below ¥300,000       | the platform                           |

Budget is not the only door into the top tier: special subject matter, a
platform's front-page push, or the producer's own declaration each open it at
any budget.

<p class="deck-source">Source: NRTA, 广电办发〔2024〕35号 — https://www.nrta.gov.cn/art/2025/2/5/art_113_70148.html</p>

```notes
The notice lists the four as alternatives, not as a budget rule with
exceptions: "'重点微短剧'（符合特殊题材、总投资额度达到100万元及以上、长短视频平台
招商主推或在各终端首页首屏推荐播出、自愿按重点微短剧申报等几种条件之一）". 几种条件
之一 — any one of them. So a cheap micro-drama that a platform pushes on its
front page is reviewed as a key one, and the table's budget column is the
common case rather than the whole rule.

Read the table as a production fact rather than as law you are being examined
on. It says that in the industry this course is imitating, a gate exists
before distribution, its threshold is a budget number, and the smallest tier's
gate is the platform's own. The exercise's quality gate is the same shape at a
scale you can run in a week.

The regulator has also published a target: a thousand good micro-dramas across
2026. Whatever that produces, it is a statement that the volume is expected to
keep going up, which is the condition a queue exists for.
```

```notes
It is the only definition of the form on this deck's source list that comes

could find. Write to the brief's own sixty-to-ninety seconds and treat the
rest as folklore.
```

## Taking a reference episode apart

![bg right:40%](./assets/cut-hook.avif)

The hook cut from this course's reference episode runs 00:00.000 to
00:03.000 — 72 frames at 24 fps. That is the whole of the window TikTok's own
guidance says a content proposition has to land inside.

<p class="deck-source">Source: TikTok Ads Manager Help, creative best practices — https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices</p>

```notes
This course keeps a one-minute reference episode specifically to take apart in
this lecture, shot by shot: which shot is the hook, where the model sheet and
master plate show up in the continuity, where a patch was clearly used instead
of a reshoot, and where the quality gate would have caught something if it had
been skipped. Watching the finished product with the queue and the gate in
mind is a different exercise than watching it as a viewer, and it's the
fastest way to see what a gate actually catches before running one on your own
footage.

The cut on the right is one of four taken from the same master, and its record
is the point of the slide as much as the picture is: a shot leaves the queue
with its timecode, its frame range and its source master attached, or it
leaves the queue unfindable.
```

## The quality gate

Continuity, loudness, captions. Captions are a text version of the speech and
non-speech audio information needed to understand the content, and they are
required at Level A. A shot that is beautiful and fails one of the three does
not ship.

<p class="deck-source">Source: W3C WAI, Captions/Subtitles — https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/captions/</p>

```notes
Nothing leaves the queue without clearing three checks: continuity against the
model sheet and master plate, loudness at a level that won't clip or vanish
next to the rest of the episode, and captions that are present and accurate. A
shot that's beautiful but fails any one of these three does not ship. The gate
exists so judgment doesn't have to be re-argued shot by shot, once a standard
has been agreed.

Captions are the one of the three with a published conformance level behind
it: WCAG success criterion 1.2.2 puts captions for prerecorded video with
audio at Level A, which is the floor rather than the aspiration. Open captions
are burnt into the picture and closed captions are carried alongside it; both
count, and which one you pick is a delivery decision.
```
