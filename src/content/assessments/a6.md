> A keyframe that can't survive its own motion isn't a keyframe yet.

## What to do

Take one subject through the five-tier ladder from this week's
exercise — sketch, flat keyframe, finished keyframe with lighting,
finished keyframe with a four-sentence motion prompt, and finally either
a first-and-last-frame pass or a reference-set-backed pass — ending on
whichever top tier the subject's motion actually needs.

## What to submit

All five clips in ladder order, the source keyframe for each, and a
one-line note on where identity held steady and where it drifted, tier
by tier.

## How it's assessed

Identity control is weighted highest: it checks whether the subject
recognisably survives its own motion at the top tier, not just at the
still keyframe underneath it. Motion-prompt precision checks whether the
four-sentence motion prompt is asking for a specific, calm change per
second rather than a vague one. Log quality checks that the log
reproduces any single tier's clip without further explanation.
