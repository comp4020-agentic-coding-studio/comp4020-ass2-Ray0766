> The critique you give is graded the same as the work you bring.

## What to do

Show up to Dailies with that week's exercise ready to screen, across all
twelve sessions, and take part in the review: not just presenting your
own work, but reading and responding to what the room brought.

## What to submit

Nothing separate. This mark is drawn from the Dailies sessions
themselves, as they happen — there is no artefact to hand in beyond
attending and contributing.

## How it's assessed

This is one holistic mark across the twelve sessions. The teaching team
records whether you attended, whether the week's exercise and its
supporting log or references were ready to screen, and a brief example
of the critique you contributed. Those records support the final
judgement; they are not twelve separate scores to average. Attendance
alone does not demonstrate preparation or useful critique.

### What makes a useful note

Locate a moment in the work, name the observation against that week's
check or reference, and propose a next action that could test or improve
it. Distinguish what you can see from what you think caused it. A useful
question can do this as well as a proposed fix, and a note about something
that worked should identify the choice worth keeping.

These examples address the same shot in the
[continuity screening](/sessions/week-08/):

- **Strong:** “At 00:06 in the second shot, the coat loses the buttons
  shown on the model sheet. Compare both shots with the full-costume
  reference. Was it included in this run? If not, include it and repeat
  the costume audit.” It locates the fault, supplies a comparison and
  gives the maker a way to judge the next attempt.
- **Adequate:** “The coat in the second shot does not match the model
  sheet.” It identifies a checkable problem, but leaves the maker to
  work out which detail changed and what to test next.
- **Weak:** “It looks wrong; use a better model.” It gives neither a
  specific observation nor a comparison the maker can act on. General
  praise has the same limitation. Comments about the person instead of
  the work do not count as critique.

A short written note shared during the screening is judged on the same
basis as a spoken contribution. Repeating a note already made adds no
evidence unless you extend it with another observation or a useful test;
the number of comments and the time spent talking are not the standard.

### How the semester judgement is made

Strong participation combines consistent preparation with specific,
evidence-based notes that help peers decide what to keep or change.
Satisfactory participation is usually prepared and identifies relevant
issues, but comparisons or next actions are uneven. Weak participation
repeatedly arrives without the required work or contributes little
beyond unsupported preferences. One excellent session does not replace
sustained participation, and one weak session does not decide the mark.

The tutor uses the screening notes to identify a next step with you:
for example, attach a reference to an observation, or make the proposed
test small enough to run. You can ask to review the participation record
and correct a missing contribution. Later sessions provide opportunities
to demonstrate improvement; the final judgement considers that development
alongside consistency across the semester. No separate reflection or
feedback portfolio is required.
