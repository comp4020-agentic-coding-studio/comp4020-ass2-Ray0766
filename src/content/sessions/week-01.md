Bring both clips, their graphs or request logs, and your timing notes —
not a screenshot, since Dailies runs from files that can be rerun, not
from what you remember happening. Each rig gets projected cold: if it
doesn't run in front of the room, that's the result, not a technical
difficulty to explain away afterward. Notes go on VRAM used or credits
spent, render time for both clips, and whether the one changed parameter
actually explains the difference between them. A rig that works but
wasn't saved as a reusable graph or log doesn't pass, and neither does one
running a checkpoint whose licence nobody in the room can name.

## What gets screened

The rig itself, not the picture it made. Two clips play, but what the
room reads is the three things standing behind them: what the run cost,
what licence the checkpoint carries, and whether the record is the kind
another person can run.

The rungs, in the order they screen:

- **the run** — it executed in front of the room, twice, on the numbers you logged
- **the licence** — someone can name it out loud, off the model card
- **the record** — hand it over and watch a stranger rerun the clip from it alone

The failure here is not an ugly shot. It is a rig that runs beautifully
and cannot be handed to anybody: no cost logged, a checkpoint nobody can
name terms for, a record that only works with its author standing next
to it.

You leave with one of the three named, and the smallest change that
fixes it before it becomes a habit.
