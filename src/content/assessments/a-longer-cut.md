> One take, several clips, no visible join.

## What to do

Produce one continuous-looking sequence, two to four minutes long, spanning
at least two distinct scenes told in live-action grammar, built from several
generated clips stitched and paced using the join techniques from Week 9.
The sequence should read as one connected piece of drama, not a reel of
individually good shots.

## What to submit

The finished sequence; the reference library from Week 8 that every shot in
it was checked against; and a production log listing each shot's seed,
parameters, and workflow graph, together with a one-line note at each scene
change explaining what signals it.

## How it's assessed

Continuity is checked against your own reference library, shot by shot, the
way Week 8's audit works. Narrative pacing covers whether beat density holds
past the opening hook and whether each scene-to-scene join reads as a
deliberate choice rather than an accident. Craft covers the human-performance
and continuity technique from Weeks 7 through 9 more generally. Production
log is checked the way Week 3's logs are: someone else should be able to see
how the sequence was built without asking.
