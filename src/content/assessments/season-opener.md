> Two episodes, and the bible that explains what a third would look like.

## What to do

Produce a series bible and the first two episodes of the season it
describes, each episode sixty to one hundred twenty seconds, framed for a
vertical screen and told in live-action grammar. Each episode needs its own
three-second hook and its own escalation, and the two episodes together need
to read as the same show — same characters, same world rules, same tone —
rather than two unrelated pieces that happen to share a bible.

## What to submit

The series bible from Week 10, revised to match what actually got produced;
both finished episodes, captions burned in; and a production log covering
every shot across both episodes, in the order the shots appear, run through
the quality gate from Week 11.

## How it's assessed

Series bible is judged on whether a third episode could plausibly be written
from it by someone who wasn't in the room. Hook and retention design covers
whether each episode's opening three seconds earns the rest of it, and
whether beat density holds across the full run. Cross-episode continuity
checks that the reference library held across both episodes without
drifting. Craft covers execution quality more generally, and production log
is checked the way every log this semester has been: legible enough to rerun
without asking.
