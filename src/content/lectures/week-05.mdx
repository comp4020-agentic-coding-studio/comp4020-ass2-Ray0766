Text to video generates motion from words alone, with nothing to anchor
composition but the prompt itself. That's weaker control than starting
from a keyframe, but it's the fastest way to test an idea before
committing an image to it, and every video generator this course uses has
a native clip length — the duration one pass reliably holds together
before coherence starts to fall apart — that the prompt has to respect
rather than fight.

## The four sentences

A usable video prompt collapses into four sentences, each doing one job:
who or what the subject is and what it's doing, how close the shot is —
the shot size, from wide to close-up — what the camera itself is doing
while the subject acts, and what the light is doing. Write those four in
order, inside the model's native clip length, and the prompt is usable
before style ever enters the conversation.

## Reading a bad take

A failed text-to-video take tends to fail in one of three specific ways:
motion that reads as physically wrong rather than merely stylised, a cut
where the model quietly changes what the subject looks like partway
through the clip, or a camera move that contradicts what the prompt asked
for. Naming which of the three happened is more useful than a general
"this doesn't look right," because each one points at a different
sentence in the prompt to fix.

## The ladder

The ladder for a text-to-video prompt runs four rising tiers, plus one
tier that is not a top but a warning. The lowest tier is one line naming
only the subject. The next tier adds the subject's action. The next tier
writes out the full four sentences — subject, shot size, camera, light.
The top tier keeps those four and adds a specific beat the action turns
on, plus a negative prompt naming what the shot must not do. And a
counter-example, not a fifth rung: the same four sentences buried under
contradictory camera and style demands at once, which reliably produces
a worse result than the plain four sentences alone — worth generating
once, on purpose, to see what over-specifying actually costs.

Pick one action you want to see performed and write it as a single
sentence, with no camera or light information yet.

## This week's exercise
