> Read your own episode the way the curve would read it.

## What to do

Present the finished pilot to the room, then write a process account
covering three things: where you'd expect the pilot's own retention
curve to drop, based on its beat sheet; one specific change you'd carry
into a bible for a second episode as a result; and a judgment on what
from this semester's process does or doesn't scale past a single
episode.

## What to submit

A one-to-two-page process account naming the predicted drop points, the
bible change they justify, and the scaling judgment, alongside the
finished pilot it's written about.

## How it's assessed

Retention analysis checks whether the predicted drop points actually
follow from the pilot's own beat sheet, not a generic guess. Bible
revision checks that the proposed change is specific enough to act on,
not a restatement of the problem. Scaling judgment checks whether the
distinction between what scales and what doesn't is argued from this
semester's own production line, not asserted. Clarity of account checks
that the whole thing is legible to someone who wasn't in the room for
the presentation.
