> Show a technique, not a stroke of luck.

## What to do

Choose one repeatable technique locked down across Weeks 1 to 3 — a
seed-and-parameter combination, a reference-frame approach, or a workflow
graph that runs on demand. Apply that one technique across six shots
spanning at least two different setups (different subjects, different
framings, or different lighting), so the portfolio demonstrates the
technique generalising rather than one lucky configuration repeated six
times.

## What to submit

Six shots, each five seconds, each accompanied by its full seed and
parameter log and the workflow graph file used to produce it. Submit the
shots as a single labelled set — shot number, setup, and one line on what
varies between this shot and the others — plus one paragraph naming the
technique and why it counts as repeatable rather than accidental.

## How it's assessed

Reproducibility carries the most weight: a marker will rerun at least one
log and expects to get back a comparable shot. Shot grammar is judged
against the four elements from Week 2 — subject, shot size, camera
movement, light. Range checks that the technique holds across genuinely
different setups, not near-duplicates. Log quality checks that the log
alone — without anyone standing next to it explaining — is enough to rerun
the shot.
